## 1. Cancer care abroad

**Is organized travel for cancer treatment used by US employer plans/insurers?**
- No US employer or insurer program sending members abroad for chemotherapy or radiation was found. Older coverage (2008–2012) of employer medical tourism (Companion Global Healthcare/BCBS South Carolina network incl. Turkey, Costa Rica; Aetna Singapore joint-replacement pilot) was orthopedic/elective, not oncology [snippet]. https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/healthcare/health-plans-begin-to-cover-medical-tourism ; https://consumerwatchdog.org/healthcare/thailand-network-employers-and-insurers-embrace-medical-tourism/
- NASH (Cancun) said in 2017 it "plans to add spine, cardiology, and oncology" [fetched, IMTJ 18 Apr 2017]; no evidence oncology ever launched. https://www.laingbuissonnews.com/imtj/news-imtj/denver-hospital-launches-travel-surgery-in-mexico/
- The real-world employer pattern for cancer is domestic COE travel: Walmart's Centers of Excellence cover "Cancer (medical record review)" at Mayo Clinic (MN, FL, AZ), with travel and lodging "Covered for you and an adult caregiver when travel is recommended," and cancer review/heart/hip-knee/transplant/spine "covered at 100%" on most plans [fetched]. https://one.walmart.com/content/usone/en_us/me/health/health-programs/centers-of-excellence.html ; expansion to most cancers (except three skin cancers) reported Oct 2024 [snippet] https://abcnews.go.com/Health/wireStory/walmart-employees-expanded-cancer-treatment-options-mayo-clinic-114420824

**Clinical objections**
- CDC Yellow Book 2026 (Stoney & Leidel, published Apr 23, 2025) [fetched]: "Oncology tourists are a vulnerable patient population." "Treatments or procedures obtained abroad may have no established benefit, placing the oncology tourist at risk for harm." "Medical tourists should discuss the plan for addressing complications related to the treatment or procedure." "Medical tourists should also know whether follow-up care is scheduled to occur at the same facility as the procedure." https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/health-care-abroad/medical-tourism.html
- Emirates Oncology Task Force consensus (PMC7348121) [snippet]: patients returning from treatment abroad often arrive with missing pathology specimens/radiology, conflicting recommendations between foreign and home oncologists, and interrupted curative radiotherapy raising treatment-failure risk (dose-compensation calculations suggested). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7348121
- Lunt & Fung 2021 scoping review ("transnational oncology treatment") lists patient safety, risk, and outcomes as under-studied [snippet]. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/178429/
- Inference (NOT VERIFIED): chemo regimens run q2–3 weeks for 4–6 months and radiation 3–7 weeks daily, so "travel for cancer" means relocation, not a trip; neutropenic fever and other acute complications need same-day local care.

## 3. Outcomes and risk

- CDC Yellow Book 2026 [fetched]: "Among medical tourists, the most common complications are infection-related." "accreditation does not guarantee a good outcome." "Medical tourists should not fly for 10 days after chest or abdominal surgery." Patients with "an overnight stay in a healthcare facility outside the United States" within 6 months should be screened for CRE. ACS: obtain "a complete set of medical records before returning home." https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/health-care-abroad/medical-tourism.html
- Bariatric tourism, UCSD, Surg Endosc 2025;39(7):4525–4532 [fetched]: 91 patients (Jan 2014–Dec 2024) treated for complications after bariatric surgery in Mexico; sleeve 63 (69.2%); leak 30 (33.0%); admission at presentation 51 (56.0%); ICU 18 (19.8%); readmission 24 (26.4%); mortality 3 (3.3%); mean charges ~$193,445/patient, payments ~$49,649 (payment-to-charge 0.27); leak cases mean $424,976. Conclusion: "Bariatric tourism can present patients and local healthcare systems with significant clinical and financial challenges." No denominator (not a rate for all Mexico surgeries). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12222374/
- Matamoros fungal meningitis, Clin Infect Dis 2024;78(6):1554–1558 (CDC authors) [fetched]: "A total of 185 residents in 22 US states and jurisdictions" at risk after epidural anesthesia at River Side Surgical Center and Clinica K-3; "11 suspected, 10 probable, and 10 confirmed US cases"; 8 deaths; Fusarium solani species complex; data as of 7 Jul 2023. News reported 11 deaths by Aug 5, 2023 [snippet]. CDC outbreak page URL tried returned 404: https://www.cdc.gov/hai/outbreaks/meningitis-epidural-anesthesia.html. https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/153479 ; https://www.krgv.com/news/death-toll-of-fungal-meningitis-infection-linked-to-matamoras-clinics-climbs-to-11/
- Dominican Republic M. abscessus: MMWR 2014;63(9):201–202 [fetched]: cosmetic surgery clinic A, "Initial antibiotic therapy was ineffective," high antibiotic resistance. EID follow-up: 21 case-patients in 6 states, 5 clinics; liposuction 74%, abdominoplasty 58%, breast implant 32%; no deaths [snippet]. Separate 2004 cluster (12 cases) and 2017 CIPLA cluster [snippet]. https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/54829 ; https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5323a4.htm ; https://doh.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/doh/page_content/attachments/NTM%20HAN%20Aug%202017%20FINAL.pdf
- Employer mitigation (NASH/Galenia, KFF Aug 12, 2019) [fetched]: US surgeon operates with Mexican surgeon; Galenia JCI-accredited; "extra autoclave," full-body gowns, PT starting hours after surgery and twice daily ~10 days before flying home; IndusHealth arranged pre-op exams, X-rays, cardiac and dental checks near home. Costs: knee replacement ~$30,000 US vs $12,000 Galenia; hospital night $300 vs ~$2,000; implant $3,500 vs ~$8,000; surgeon paid $2,700. Ashley Furniture: no copay/deductible, $5,000 payment, travel covered; saved $3.2M since 2016; ~140 employees/dependents to Costa Rica, Ferguson the 10th to Cancun; NASH "a couple dozen" patients since 2017. https://kffhealthnews.org/news/to-save-money-american-patients-and-surgeons-meet-in-cancun/
- NASH status 2025–2026: nothing found; all coverage 2016–2019 [search]. NOT VERIFIED whether still operating.

## 4. Follow-up care

- NASH/IMTJ [fetched]: "Pre- and post-operative care is delivered in the US"; surgeons "on a rotational basis in Cancun." KFF: article says patients should ask how care is handed off after return but describes no specific arrangement [fetched].
- UCSD authors recommend "obtaining complete records from foreign centers, and arranging follow-up with an accredited bariatric program at home" [fetched].
- Telemedicine licensing [snippet, law-firm commentary]: patient location controls; a physician treating a patient located in another state (or country) generally needs a license there; NY allows out-of-state physicians to consult with a NY-licensed physician under limited conditions; Interstate Medical Licensure Compact and Uniform Telehealth Act are US-physician solutions, not foreign-physician ones. Inference: a Mexican/Indian surgeon cannot lawfully conduct follow-up telemedicine with a NJ patient absent NJ licensure; follow-up must be a US physician or the same US surgeon (the NASH design). https://www.law360.com/articles/1260950 ; https://www.gtlaw.com/-/media/files/insights/published-articles/020810_new-york-law-journal-telemedicine-legal-and-practical-considerations.pdf ; https://msuilr.org/msuilr-legalforum-blogs/2016/3/7/rr0qjfhxl3wzcbzhzeefjqdvp7fojv
- Malpractice: "NASH buys additional malpractice coverage for the American physicians, who could be sued in the United States by patients unhappy with their results" [fetched, KFF]. I. Glenn Cohen (Harvard): "clever strategy ... It doesn't answer all concerns" [snippet]. Cohen's book Patients with Passports (OUP 2015) is the standard legal treatment [snippet]. https://hls.harvard.edu/bibliography/patients-with-passports-medical-tourism-law-and-ethics

**ERISA/ACA/HIPAA**
- Venable, Jul 20, 2022 [fetched]: a medical travel benefit "is a group health plan or part of one because reimbursement depends on receiving medical care"; "A company cannot simply amend its corporate travel reimbursement policy"; must be in a plan document and SPD with ERISA claims/appeals procedures and generally Form 5500; improperly designed benefit violates the ACA with "$100 per affected person per day"; HIPAA privacy/security apply unless self-administered with fewer than 50 employees; stand-alone designs allowed only for enrollees or if not providing "substantial benefits in the nature of medical care." https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2022/07/medical-travel-benefits-what-employers-need-to
- Carrum Health COE plan documents (Maine, Tehama County): "a portion of the covered travel expenses will be reported as taxable income to the employee" [snippet]. https://www.maine.gov/bhr/oeh/sites/maine.gov.bhr.oeh/files/inline-files/COE%20Listing.pdf
- Wellness incentives, 78 FR 33158 (Jun 3, 2013) [fetched]: raises health-contingent reward "from 20 percent to 30 percent of the cost of coverage" and "to 50 percent" for tobacco programs; reward "must not exceed 30 percent of the total cost of employee-only coverage"; five requirements (annual opportunity, size cap, reasonable alternative standard for all similarly situated, reasonably designed, disclosure); participatory programs (no health-standard condition) are not capped; effective Aug 2, 2013, plan years on/after Jan 1, 2014. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-06-03/html/2013-12916.htm
- Not found: any authority treating a travel-surgery incentive (waived deductible, cash payment) as a HIPAA nondiscrimination issue; practice (Ashley, Walmart, state Carrum plans) suggests it is treated as plan design, not a wellness reward. NOT VERIFIED.

**Tax (IRS Pub 502, for 2025 returns)** [fetched]
- Lodging includible only if "primarily for and essential to medical care," by a doctor in a licensed hospital or equivalent, not "lavish or extravagant," and no significant element of personal pleasure; capped at $50 per night per person, $100 with a companion; meals not included. Transportation includible (bus, taxi, train, plane, ambulance; parent accompanying child). Car: actual gas/oil or "21 cents a mile" (2025) plus parking/tolls. Not includible: trips "merely for a change in environment, improvement of morale, or general improvement of health ... even if the trip is made on the advice of a doctor." https://www.irs.gov/publications/p502

## 6. Prevention with evidence

- DPP trial (NEJM 2002;346:393–403): 3,234 participants; lifestyle arm incidence reduced 58% (95% CI 48–66) vs placebo, metformin 31%, mean 2.8 yr follow-up [snippet]. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/
- CMS DPP actuarial fact sheet (Mar 23, 2016) [fetched]: 17 YMCAs; 6,874 recruited / 5,696 enrolled as of Mar 2015; "statistically significant savings of $2,650 for each enrollee over the first 5 quarters"; ~9 lb loss with 4+ core sessions, ~11.7 lb at one year with a maintenance session; Office of the Actuary concluded expansion would save Medicare money. https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/diabetes-prevention-program-independent-evaluation-report-summary
- RTI/Health Affairs (Mar 6, 2017) [fetched]: 3,319 Medicare participants; "$278 per member per quarter over the first three years"; fewer admissions/ED visits; ~9 lb loss; "Savings were highest during people's first year of participation and decreased after the program ended." https://www.rti.org/news/ymca-diabetes-prevention-program-helps-medicare-beneficiaries-avoid-hospital-visits-and-saves
- MDPP (Medicare benefit) [snippet]: average 4.9–5.1% weight loss; >half reach 5%; 25% reach 9%; those meeting 5% had 36% lower diabetes incidence than non-achievers (RTI). CMS 2025 final evaluation report is a 6.2 MB PDF that WebFetch could not parse: https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/data-and-reports/2025/mdpp-finalevalrpt ; https://rti.org/announcements/new-report-evaluates-lifestyle-intervention-impact-development-type-2-diabetes
- Walking groups, Hanson & Jones, Br J Sports Med 2015 [snippet]: 42 studies, 1,843 adults; systolic BP -3.72 mmHg, diastolic -3.14; depression effect size -0.67; "No notable adverse side effects"; good adherence. https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/document/12625
- Social connection: 2026 meta-analysis of 19 RCTs, loneliness SMD -0.60 (95% CI -1.00 to -0.20), I² 93% [snippet]; no walking-group-specific or hard-outcome evidence found. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12819595/
- Wellness ROI, BJ's Wholesale (Song & Baicker): JAMA Apr 2019 at 18 months — more self-reported exercise and weight management, no differences in clinical measures, spending, absenteeism, or tenure [snippet]. Health Affairs Jun 2021 three-year follow-up (403'd: https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2020.01808): "no significant differences were found in self-reported health; clinical markers of health; health care spending or use; or absenteeism, tenure, or job performance"; only self-reported behaviors improved (~11% higher share actively managing weight) [snippet]. https://kffhealthnews.org/news/workplace-wellness-programs-barely-move-the-needle-study-finds/ ; https://benefitspro.com/2021/06/23/wellness-program-study-shows-mixed-findings-after-three-years
- Illinois Workplace Wellness (NBER page) [fetched]: QJE 2019 — participants "had lower medical expenditures" pre-program (selection); "we do not find significant causal effects of treatment on total medical expenditures"; "Our 95 percent confidence intervals rule out 84 percent of previous estimates on medical spending and absenteeism"; screening rates rose. JAMA Intern Med 2020 (n=4,834) — "no significant effects of the program on biometrics, medical diagnoses, or medical use were seen after 12 or 24 months"; more had a primary care physician (92.2% vs 86.1%). https://www.nber.org/programs-projects/projects-and-centers/workplace-wellness/illinois-workplace-wellness-results
