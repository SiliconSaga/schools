# Law notes: OPRA and special-education rules

Gathered September 30, 2026 by a research agent through a summarizing fetch tool. Section numbers and quotes have **not** been read against the primary text by a person. Check each against its link before it is cited on the public site. None of this is legal advice.

Primary sources: OPRA as posted by the Government Records Council, https://www.nj.gov/grc/laws/act/ ; the 2024 amendments, P.L. 2024, c.16, https://pub.njleg.gov/Bills/2024/AL24/16_.HTM (effective September 3, 2024).

## OPRA

- **Deadline.** N.J.S.A. 47:1A-5(i): grant or deny "as soon as possible, but not later than seven business days after receiving the request"; 14 for commercial requests. A review comment reports that the 2024 text also allows 14 business days where records in storage must be reviewed for Daniel's Law (P.L. 2021, c.371) redactions; not yet checked against the statute. Failure to respond "shall be deemed a denial", unless the requester did not accurately identify themselves.
- **Immediate access.** 47:1A-5(e): "Immediate access ordinarily shall be granted to budgets, bills, vouchers, contracts, including collective negotiations agreements and individual employment contracts, and public employee salary and overtime information." Not required for documents over 24 months old.
- **Form.** 47:1A-5(f), (g): custodians adopt the GRC's form; "a letter, or an email from a requestor including all of the information required on the adopted form shall suffice". The form's fields: name, address, email, phone, a brief description of the record, a commercial-purpose certification, and a certification "whether the government record is being sought in connection with a legal proceeding". A requester may leave the identity fields blank (see the next item) at the cost of any remedy.
- **Anonymous requests.** Allowed, but an anonymous requester cannot bring a challenge, and non-response is not a deemed denial.
- **Correspondence.** A custodian need not complete a request for mail, email, texts, or social media messages "if the request does not identify a specific job title or accounts to be searched, a specific subject matter, and is not confined to a reasonable time period". Identifying people by job title is sufficient.
- **No research, no new records.** Now in the statute, 47:1A-5(g). Cases: MAG Entertainment v. Div. of ABC, 375 N.J. Super. 534 (App. Div. 2005) ("OPRA does not countenance open-ended searches of an agency's files"); Paff v. Galloway Twp., 229 N.J. 340 (2017) (extracting electronically stored information "is not the creation of a new record"). How Paff sits with the 2024 research clause is undecided.
- **Personnel records.** 47:1A-10: exempt except name, title, position, salary, payroll record, length of service, date of separation and the reason. Whether this governs a vendor's employees is unverified.
- **Student records.** N.J.A.C. 6A:32-7.5(g) (as amended 2022): a district "may release, without consent, records removed of all personally identifiable information". Earlier case: L.R. v. Camden City Pub. Sch. Dist., 452 N.J. Super. 56 (App. Div. 2017), aff'd by an equally divided court, 238 N.J. 547 (2019).
- **Deliberative material.** 47:1A-1.1 excludes "inter-agency or intra-agency advisory, consultative, or deliberative material"; test in Education Law Center v. NJDOE, 198 N.J. 274 (2009).
- **Records held by third parties.** Burnett v. County of Gloucester, 415 N.J. Super. 506 (App. Div. 2010) concerned settlement agreements held by the county's insurers, made "by or on behalf of" the agency. Whether it reaches a staffing vendor's internal timesheets or fill reports is not established. Records the contract obliges the vendor to deliver are the safer target.
- **Fees.** 47:1A-5(b)(1): electronic records free of charge. Special service charge, 5(c): actual direct cost, reasonable, with an itemized explanation.
- **Remedies.** 47:1A-6: within 45 days of denial, Superior Court or the Government Records Council; the agency bears the burden. Since 2024 a prevailing requester "may be entitled" to attorney's fees; fees are mandatory only where the agency unreasonably denied access, acted in bad faith, or knowingly and willfully violated the law.
- **Recent.** Rosetti v. Ramapo-Indian Hills Reg'l H.S. Bd. of Educ. (N.J. June 11, 2026) reportedly held that logs of board-related emails in members' personal accounts are government records. Secondary reports only.

## Special education

- **State complaint.** N.J.A.C. 6A:14-9.2: "An organization or individual may request a complaint investigation by simultaneously submitting a signed, written request to the Director of the Office and to the public agency". One-year lookback; decision within 60 calendar days. The signed complaint is filed as a PDF to specialeducationcomplaints@doe.nj.gov, with a copy served on the district; exhibits and supporting documents are not accepted by email and go by mail or fax. https://www.nj.gov/education/specialed/policy/disputeresolution/
- **IEP delivery.** N.J.A.C. 6A:14-3.7(a): "The district board of education shall ensure that there is no delay in implementing a student's IEP." 34 CFR 300.323(c)(2).
- **Aides.** N.J.A.C. 6A:14-4.1(e): a classroom aide works under the direction of certified personnel, and "The job description of a classroom aide shall be approved by the Department of Education through the county office of education." 34 CFR 300.156(b)(2)(iii): paraprofessionals must be "appropriately trained and supervised".
- **Contracting out.** No rule found barring agency aides. N.J.S.A. 18A:6-7.1 requires criminal history checks for contracted staff in regular contact with pupils. N.J.S.A. 34:13A-44 et seq. (P.L. 2020, c.79) limits subcontracting during an existing collective agreement and requires notice. N.J.A.C. 6A:32-4.7 was repealed in 2013; do not cite it.
- **Field trips and nonacademic activities.** 34 CFR 104.4(a) and 104.37(a)(1) (Section 504): activities provided "in such manner as is necessary to afford handicapped students an equal opportunity for participation". 34 CFR 300.107 and 300.117 (IDEA): supplementary aids and services in nonacademic settings as the IEP team determines. N.J.A.C. 6A:14-4.2(b) and 6A:14-3.7(e). The federal Office for Civil Rights letters on field-trip exclusion were seen in search summaries only.
- **Compensatory education.** M.C. v. Central Regional Sch. Dist., 81 F.3d 389 (3d Cir. 1996): owed for the period of deprivation, less the time reasonably needed to fix it. 34 CFR 300.151(b) for state-complaint remedies.
