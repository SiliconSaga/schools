## Notes for you (not part of the request)

1. **Why this is its own request.** "When is there room for a full-length movie" is a question, and OPRA answers requests for documents, not questions. The documents that bear on it are the schedules (item 1), the rule the district set for itself (item 2), and the two most likely causes: classes with no teacher or substitute (item 3) and anything staff were told (item 4). Item 4 is correspondence, the category most likely to draw an extension, so keeping it out of OPRA-001 and OPRA-002 keeps those two fast.
2. **What the other parent's request returned.** The September 11 response in this folder is the district's course roster as reported to the state: 38 pages of course codes with enrollment by grade for every school. It has no times, durations, or schedules, so item 1 does not duplicate it.
3. **Check the policy online first.** If the district's policy manual is posted, item 2 may already be public. Cite the policy number in the request if you find it, or drop the item.
4. **Optional, and pointed.** Showing a movie in class as part of teaching is covered by copyright law's classroom exemption; showing one as entertainment generally needs a public performance license. A fifth item could ask for "any public performance license for motion pictures held by the District or any school for 2026-27". It is a fair records question, but it reads as adversarial; add it only if you want that edge.
5. **What you know first-hand matters more than this request.** Dates, grades, and which movie, written down the day your children mention it, are the "report" level of evidence. Two or more parents noting the same day at the same school turns it into something the ledger can describe.
6. **The substitute vendor.** The August 24 minutes reportedly approve Sunbelt Staffing, LLC for substitutes for 2026-27. I have not read that page myself, so the request says "the substitute staffing vendor" rather than naming it.
