# How the Pay Works

The contract dispute and the outsourcing are both arguments about pay, and the words in them (guide, step, column, longevity, stipend, per diem, markup) are rarely explained. This page explains them from the district's own documents, with every number linked. It takes no position on what anyone should be paid.

The current agreement between the Board and the West Orange Education Association (WOEA) ran from July 1, 2020 to June 30, 2025 and has not been posted online by the district or the union as far as I can find; a successor is under negotiation. The most recent WOEA agreement in the state's public file is the one for [2004-2007](https://www.perc.state.nj.us/publicsectorcontracts.nsf/Contracts%20By%20Employer/FAEF90B1943630BB8525727B00722158/$File/W%20Orange%20BE%20and%20W%20Orange%20EA%202004.pdf), kept by the Public Employment Relations Commission. Where this page describes structure, it uses that agreement and says so; where it gives 2026 figures, it uses the lists the Board approved on [May 4, 2026](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1777924001/woboeorg/tacqxglh2ky5ceo8hyp3/UPDATEDMay42026AgendaAttachments5-4-26.pdf).

## The salary guide

A teacher's base salary is read off a table called the salary guide. Rows are **steps**, which count years of service; columns are **training levels**, which track degrees and credits. The 2004-2007 agreement's guide (Schedule A) had steps 1 through 10 and eight columns: BA, BA+16, BA+32, MA, MA+16, MA+32, MA+48, and DR. In 2006-2007 it ran from $42,561 (step 1, BA) to $88,964 (step 10, DR). Each year a teacher normally moves down one step; finishing a degree or a block of credits moves them across a column. Reaching the last step is called being at the top of the guide, and from then on the only increases are the across-the-board raises negotiated in each new contract. The agreement also says that "Increases on all guides shall be based on meritorious service" and that "There shall be no automatic increments or increase in salary" (Article VI.A.4, p. 21; the same words apply to instructional assistants at p. 28).

**What the 2026-27 list shows.** The reappointment list for tenured WOEA certificated staff (agenda attachments, pp. 11-32) gives each person's base salary for 2026-27. For the 545 people on it, base salary runs from $42,336.50 to $154,941.25, with a median of $111,038; 332 of the 545 are at $100,000 or more. The single most common figure is $123,953, shared by 128 people, with $122,253 next at 67, which is consistent with a large group sitting at the top step of the most common columns. Base salaries on the list total $57,012,755. These are tenured staff, so new and non-tenured teachers, who are listed separately (pp. 33-37) and sit lower on the guide, are not in these figures.

## Longevity

Longevity is a fixed amount added to salary each year once a teacher has completed a set number of years in West Orange. It is not a raise on the guide; it is a separate line, and it is the only service-based increase available to someone already at the top of the guide. In the 2004-2007 agreement (Article VI.D, p. 29) it had four tiers, paid "for each school year, prorated from the relevant service completion anniversary date":

| Years of service completed | 2006-07 amount |
|---|---|
| 14 to 18 | $4,287 |
| 19 to 23 | $5,517 |
| 24 to 28 | $6,753 |
| 29 and more | $8,000 |

**What the 2026-27 list shows.** The same May 4 list has a longevity column. Of the 545 tenured staff, 320 have a longevity amount and 225 have none. The amounts are $5,612 (86 people), $7,221 (169), $8,840 (50), and $10,471 (12), plus three pro-rated amounts under $5,000. Each of the four is 1.309 times the corresponding 2006-07 tier, which suggests the four-tier structure has been carried forward with the contract raises since; the current years-of-service thresholds are not public, so they are not stated here. Longevity on the list totals $2,280,577.60, which is 3.8% of the $59,293,333 in total salary on the same list. The tally is reproducible with a [short script](https://github.com/SiliconSaga/schools/blob/main/_research/tools/longevity-tally.py).

Longevity follows seniority, and so does base salary, so the two line up: of the 208 people with base salaries between $120,000 and $130,000, 183 have longevity; of the 67 between $70,000 and $80,000, six do. The committee's October 14 email said longevity "is not widely provided to teachers working in other school districts across New Jersey"; the New Jersey School Boards Association's magazine reported in 2025 that about three-quarters of districts provide it ([School Leader, October 2025](https://www.njsba.org/news-information/school-leader/trends-in-teacher-contract-negotiations/)). Both claims are set beside each other on [What the District Said](/ledger/statements#longevity-pay).

## Stipends and extra duty

Coaching, club advising, and similar assignments carry stipends set in separate schedules of the agreement (Schedules D and E in 2004-2007). Routine extra duties have per-diem rates: the May 4 agenda approved "A.M. / P.M. Bus Duty", "Breakfast / Lunch Duty", and "General Detention" at "$40.81 per diem" as "the WOEA contractual rates" (agenda attachments, p. 4). None of the 545 tenured staff on the reappointment list has a stipend on that list; stipends are approved in separate resolutions through the year.

## Hourly rates

Summer work is paid by the hour, and the 2026 Extended School Year assignments the Board approved on May 4 give the district's hourly rates for the same jobs side by side (agenda attachments, pp. 57-67): teachers $57.13, paraprofessionals $26.82, nurses $62.89, clerical aides $22.01, and substitute teachers and substitute paraprofessionals at the same $57.13 and $26.82. A paraprofessional hour is 47% of a teacher hour at these rates.

## Paraprofessionals

Paraprofessionals (the 2004-2007 agreement calls them instructional assistants) were in the WOEA unit with their own guide, Schedule C: ten steps and three columns (Non-Deg, BA, MA), running in 2006-2007 from $22,101 to $28,437 a year, with longevity of $726 to $1,815 on the same four tiers as teachers. The May 4, 2026 attachments contain reappointment lists for certificated staff, administrators, administrative assistants, buildings and grounds, security, transportation, and technology, but no list of paraprofessionals, which is consistent with the decision to outsource the positions that same spring. The $26.82 summer rate above is the only 2026 district figure for paraprofessional pay on a public document found so far.

What a district employee costs beyond salary is pension (the Public Employees' Retirement System for paraprofessionals, the Teachers' Pension and Annuity Fund for teachers), the employer's share of Social Security and Medicare, and health coverage. A staffing agency's employee gets the wage and whatever the agency provides. The Superintendent's April 29 letter put the saving from outsourcing at $3 million a year; which of these lines the figure comes from has not been shown. [OPRA-001](/ledger/opra/001-edustaff) asks for the agreement and the bills.

## Substitutes and the agency markup

Day-to-day substitutes are paid a per-diem rate, and since 2022 the district has obtained them through EduStaff rather than hiring them directly. Under the [January 2022 master agreement](/ledger/opra/files/edustaff-master-agreement-2022-01-13.pdf), EduStaff is the employer: it pays the substitute and invoices the district for the wages plus a fee of "30.00% of Staff Gross Payroll" for classroom-based substitute staff and 33% for school nurses (Exhibit B). So a substitute paid $110 for a day is billed to the district at $143. The fee rises if employer payroll taxes rise by more than half a percent of payroll, and the district owes $500 if it hires one of the agency's substitutes onto its own payroll. The agreement's invoices are to be itemized by job number, person covered, and hours or days worked, in PDF and Excel (Section 4.B and Exhibit A). For 2026-27 the Board also approved a substitute staffing agreement with Sunbelt Staffing on August 24, 2026 ([minutes, p. 12](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1790700674/woboeorg/zv51wsqdw2coq5gaafqr/August242026-Minutes.pdf)).

Whether the paraprofessionals now supplied through EduStaff are priced on the same markup, and at what hourly wage, is what the July 21 "Pricing Schedule Addendum-Exhibit B" would show. It is the first item in OPRA-001.
